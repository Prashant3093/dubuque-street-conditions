- Observations used: 18,087

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 81 | 73 | 69 | 71 | 78 | age 4 (-1.83 pts/yr) |
| Asphalt | 81 | 73 | 67 | 66 | 67 | age 4 (-1.84 pts/yr) |
| Concrete | 97 | 92 | 90 | 91 | 87 | age 6 (-1.31 pts/yr) |

- The pooled curve flattens and even rises after ~age 10. That is not pavement healing: older segments are disproportionately concrete, and the ones still unrepaired at age 20+ are the survivors that never needed work. Read the material-specific curves, and only the first ~10–15 years with confidence.
- Median age to Poor (Kaplan-Meier, 2,775 segments, 371 reached Poor): **not reached** — fewer than half of segments are observed falling to Poor within the ages the data covers.
- Share of segments still above Poor at age: 10 yrs 96%, 15 yrs 92%, 20 yrs 81%, 25 yrs 75%
  - Asphalt (ACC): median 26 years (325 reached Poor)
  - Concrete (PCC): median not reached (36 reached Poor)
- Caveats: the 2015–2020 gap means the first observed Poor rating is an upper bound on when it happened. Ages beyond ~20 come mostly from segments that have *not* yet been repaired, i.e. survivors.
